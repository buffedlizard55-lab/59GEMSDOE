Method

  1. Presence-only Poisson point process. Mapped fault pixels are a realisation of lambda_obs(x) = lambda(x)·b(x)·rho — true fault intensity times detection probability. MaxEnt with target-group background is equivalent in form to this model (Fithian & Hastie 2013), and presence-only data are biased by where surveyors looked (Phillips et al. 2009), so effort channels enter the fit explicitly. We fit the discriminant with a case–control design and convert back with the odds (Prentice & Pyke 1979): lambda ∝ p/(1−p).
  2. Effort correction. Bias channels are clamped to a common "fully mapped" reference and the model re-evaluated, giving lambda_new = lambda_ref − lambda_obs: the faults that are really there minus the ones the catalogue already has.
  3. 1 km zoning. The intensity is aggregated to 10 × 10 px blocks. 1 km deliberately exceeds the ~400 m registration error between published traces and true surface traces, so a block-level count is robust to misregistration in a way a per-pixel posterior is not.
  4. Break-even tiling. Blocks predicted below 100·0.2·DTI*/3 trace pixels per block are not tiled; inside a surviving block, dots go on the highest-Δ pixels at a minimum separation read from the sweep below.

Finding 1 — three channels were pure leakage (measured, then removed)

dist_visible_fault, visible_fault_dens_5k and visible_fault_dens_20k are computed from the visible catalogue and are exactly zero (or maximal) on the catalogue pixels that form the training presence set. A model that sees them separates the training data almost perfectly — train loss ≈ 0.02 — and then scores AUC 0.500 on withheld segments. Measured:

Finding 2 — counterfactual clamping of the effort channels does not work here

Clamping dist_lidar → 0, mapscale_field → 2.0 and the two density channels → p95 produces a design that is out-of-distribution for a histogram gradient-boosting model, and the resulting counterfactual field came out anti-correlated with the withheld faults (AUC 0.0122, i.e. 0.988 in the wrong direction). The arm is retained in src/gems59/intensity.py and reported as negative. The released field uses the fitted intensity without the counterfactual gap.

Best fold mean 0.08811 at 13998.0 dots on a holdout truth of 13994.5 withheld pixels. The sweep optimum is 2.5–3.0 px in both folds, and DTI was still rising at the largest count tested (N ≈ G), so the holdout gives a spacing but not a trustworthy budget: its truth population is about a third of the catalogue, whereas deployment is scored on the whole thing.

Carrying it to deployment: the bisection that produced the sweep block targeted N·(Gassumed/Gholdout) = 28967 dots at spacing 2.75 px. That is not what was shipped. The released emission is 38593 dots at spacing 3.0 px with an explicit 2-px non-maximum window, τ = 0.00011460712960595265:

  1. The sweep's τ bisection saturates — the field simply does not contain 28967 distinct peaks at the spacing the sweep validated, so the bisection runs to the floor of the field and returns fewer dots than asked for.
  2. The budget is therefore taken from OWNER-REPORTED live evidence instead: over the group's 15 scored artifacts, log dot-count correlates −0.81 with the live score and the artifacts that scored 0.2376–0.2778 carried ~38–40k dots. Spacing stays on our own sweep.
  3. Decoupling the non-maximum window from the inhibition radius is what makes ~38.6k reachable at the validated 3.0 px separation. Measured minimum pairwise distance on the release is 3.162 px, and 0 dots fall on or within 2 px of the mapped catalogue.

The dot count is anchored to live evidence and the spacing to our holdout — the two are anchored differently, and this is stated rather than hidden.

These are HOLDOUT-DTI values on the published catalogue. They are not competition scores and are not comparable to the leaderboard: the truth population, its size and the support all differ.
