## 3. Why that geometry wins under the official metric (DERIVED)

The official metric is `DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)` with a 300 m (3 px) triangular kernel
(problem page 967; implementation `src/gems_dti/metric.py`, transcribed and unit-tested here).
Because `TP_w + FN_w = G` exactly, `DTI = TP_w / (0.2·S_w + 0.8·G)` with `S_w = TP_w + FP_w`, and adding
one non-redundant dot of kernel credit `k` raises the score iff

  **k > 0.2 · DTI**  (proof in `docs/research/utility-derivation.md`; algebra verified 500/500 synthetic)

At the 0.2778 level the bar is `k > 0.0556`, i.e. **a dot pays only if it lands within ≈ 2.83 px
(283 m) of a truth pixel**. Three consequences decide the whole competition:

1. **Dot count is the primary risk.** Every dot that misses the 283 m corridor costs the same as a
   truthful dot earns. A sparse, high-precision set beats a dense, low-precision one at equal hit rate.
2. **Dots on already-mapped faults are free but worthless.** DrivenData staff (2026-09-16) confirmed
   that known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are masked out of scoring entirely, so a dot there neither
   earns nor costs. The "prune" therefore removes only the *flanks* (unmasked cells 1–2 px off a known
   trace, which can never be truth and always cost).
3. **Coverage lattices need the tiled area to contain most of the truth.** If a block lattice covers a
   fraction `a` of the truth and the dots are placed on a 4 px lattice, `TP ≈ 0.44·a·G`; with
   `G ≈ 30,000`, `n = 35,000` that is `DTI ≈ 0.05` unless `a` is very large. This is exactly why the
   family's *block-tiled* designs stall around 0.25–0.28 while the point-precision designs advance.

## 4. The measurement that changes everything: our own holdout is inverted

Two instruments were calibrated this session against the 24 rasters whose public score the owner
recorded (`scripts/exp_live_calibration.py`, `scripts/exp_corridor_calibration.py`; evidence
`evidence/h59j_live_calibration.json`, `evidence/h59j_corridor_calibration.json`):

| instrument | Spearman vs recorded public score | p | n |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| shared hide-and-recover holdout, `gems57-pooled-hide-v2`, seed 20 | **−0.437** | 0.033 | 24 |
| same, file-name-tier scores only | **−0.483** | 0.058 | 16 |
| corridor-restricted variant (truth ≥ 6 px from any visible trace) | −0.427 | 0.038 | 24 |
| corridor-restricted variant (truth ≥ 10 px) | −0.383 | 0.064 | 24 |
| **log10(emitted dots)** | **−0.818** | < 1e-4 | 24 |
| log10(emitted dots), file-name tier | **−0.859** | < 1e-4 | 16 |

The mechanism is structural, not statistical noise: the hide-and-recover truth *is* the mapped
catalogue, so (a) designs that decorate the visible traces score on it, and (b) a design that prunes
its dots away from the catalogue — the very thing the live scorer rewards — collapses on it
(the 0.2778 raster scores `TP_w = 59.7` out of 11,321 withheld truth pixels on that instrument, i.e.
DTI 0.0037, essentially last of the 24).

**Irregularity for review (IR-59J-01):** `REMAINING_WORK.md` §1 called this the "central unresolved
tension"; it is now measured, and it invalidates the promotion gate that required a candidate to beat
the 0.135 holdout best. Any candidate judged by that gate is being judged by an instrument that ranks
the field's best recorded submission near the bottom.

## 5. Answering the standing question: can we beat 0.2778?

**Not proven, and not provable from this sandbox** (no DrivenData account, no private labels, no GPU).
What the evidence *does* support:

* The recorded score is a monotone decreasing function of emitted dot count across every design family
  in the owner's own record — `264k → 0.034`, `207k 5-px lattice → 0.090`, `155k → 0.083–0.119`,
  `121k → 0.192`, `60k → 0.248`, `42k → 0.265`, `40k → 0.271`, `37.7k → 0.271/0.278`.
* The leaderboard's 0.41-class entries sit 0.10–0.13 above the best sibling design; closing that gap
  needs either a materially better *place*-ment model (a trained CNN on the 19 bands, or the 1 m DEM
  that this sandbox cannot download) or a materially better *geometry*. Nothing in the local evidence
  says the geometry is exhausted: the 0.2778 raster only covers 13 % of 1 km blocks with 2.4 dots each.
* Therefore the defensible next step is a **precision-first, budget-capped emission** whose geometry is
  validated against the live-calibrated relation (dot count) while its *placement* is stated as a
  projection — which is what the H59-J candidate shipped alongside this memo does
  (`docs/downloads/gems59-h59j-coherence-consensus-dotted-20261011T043000Z.tif`).

**Projection, explicitly not a score.** A design in the 30–38k-dot band, built from catalogue-independent
lineament evidence with a 2 px catalogue collar, would be expected to land in the same 0.24–0.29 band as
the recorded siblings of that size; a materially higher position requires placement skill this project
has not yet demonstrated. No local number on any page of this site is a competition score.
